# Openly-licensed photo candidates: ska/rocksteady/reggae/2 Tone/dancehall login background

Research date: 2026-09-24. Scope: dubplate login page background. Every candidate below
was verified on its actual Wikimedia Commons (or Flickr) source page — license text is
quoted, not inferred. CC BY-SA entries are flagged (share-alike carries redistribution
obligations for the dubplate binary; still permitted per the brief, but noted).

**Headline finding:** the openly-licensed photographic record of this whole subject area is
very thin. Wikimedia Commons' Nationaal Archief/Anefo trove (CC0/CC BY-SA, the strongest
single source in the brief) documents almost none of the first-wave Jamaican ska/rocksteady
artists — extensive direct searches (`Anefo Byron Lee`, `Anefo Desmond Dekker`, `Anefo Jimmy
Cliff`, `Anefo Prince Buster`, `Anefo Toots Maytals`, `Anefo Bob Marley`) returned **zero**
hits. The sole confirmed Anefo/period hit for a Jamaican ska-era musician is Millie Small's
1964 Schiphol arrival. Everything else usable is either: (a) a modern CC-licensed photo of a
veteran artist performing decades later, which is out of scope for a "vintage 1960s/70s
photography" brief and is listed separately as excluded/out-of-era, or (b) genuinely absent.
The same pattern holds for the two subjects specifically added mid-task (see their own
sections below): the professional documentary photography of both the original 1969
multiracial skinhead scene and the 1979–85 2 Tone/early-dancehall/early-US-ska scene remains
under agency/estate copyright; only much later fan/reunion-tour snapshots are CC-licensed.

Fit score: 1 (weak, posed headshot) to 5 (strong moody full-bleed atmosphere).

## 2. Multiracial 1969-era British skinhead scene (owner priority add)

**Verified count: 0.** After specifically searching Commons full-text (`skinhead 1969`, `skinhead
reggae`, `Trojan Records` + skinhead, `Suedeheads and trojan skins`), Commons categories
(Category:Skinheads and its files), and the Wikipedia articles most likely to embed a period
Commons image (`Skinhead`, `Trojan skinhead`), no genuine 1969–71 photograph of the original
multiracial, music-centered skinhead scene turned up under any redistribution-safe license.

What exists instead, all confirmed non-period:
- **Suedeheads and trojan skins 46670.jpg** — dated 2009-02-15, Flickr user `skinhead_reggae`, CC BY 2.0 — [source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Suedeheads_and_trojan_skins_46670.jpg). A 2009 photo of trad-skin revivalists, tiny (200×310). Fit 2 if 1969-look mattered, but it is not period.
- **Leeds skinheads (6778923625).jpg** — dated 2012-01-29, photographer Bryan Ledgard, CC BY 2.0 — [source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leeds_skinheads_(6778923625).jpg). Modern street-photography series, not 1969.
- **Hoxton Tom McCourt 1977.jpg**, **WLANL - petertf - Compartments II, Graham Dean (1979).jpg**, **Skinheads in Cuba 1994.jpg**, **Skinheads in London City in 1981.jpg** — all reviewed; all post-1972 (the SHARP/oi!/punk-adjacent second wave or later), several ambiguous/adjacent to far-right skinhead imagery from the 1980s onward and explicitly excluded on that basis regardless of date.
- Wikipedia's own `Skinhead` article illustrates its "Origins" section with **SHARP_skinhead.jpg**, a 1990s-2000s anti-racist-skinhead photo, not an original-scene image; `Trojan_skinhead` illustrates with the 1981 London file above.

**Assessment:** the professional documentary photographers who actually shot the 1969 scene
(Homer Sykes, Janette Beckman-adjacent contemporaries, and later Gavin Watson's retrospective
archive) hold their work under conventional copyright via agencies/estates — none of it is
CC-licensed or public domain (see the off-limits list, section 4). Museum of Youth Culture's
online holdings on this subject are also rights-managed, not open-licensed. This subject
should be treated as **unavailable under the license bar**, not merely under-searched; a
follow-up would need to contact rights holders directly rather than search further.

## 3. Early-1980s 2 Tone, early Jamaican dancehall, and early American ska (owner scope widening)

**Verified count: 0** genuinely period (1979–85) photographs found under an open license, across
2 Tone bands, Jamaican dancehall, and US ska. Every Commons category for the named acts was
checked directly:

- **Category:The Specials** — files are album art, informational plaques at the old Woodbine
  Street studio, and live/portrait photos dated 2009, 2013, 2015, 2019, 2022. Nothing from
  1979–81.
- **Category:Madness (band)** — earliest dated file is 2005; the rest run 2008–2024.
- **Category:Bad Manners** — 32 files, all from a single modern show (Flickr IDs in the
  51,4xx,xxx,xxx range, i.e. very recent uploads); no 1979–85 material and no visible per-file
  license/date without opening each page, but the dates are unambiguous placeholders for "recent."
- **The Selecter, The Bodysnatchers, The Beat**: no dedicated Commons category with period
  content found; not pursued further given the pattern above.
- **Category:Yellowman** (Jamaican dancehall pioneer) — 14 files, dated 2007, 2018, 2022, 2024.
  Nothing from Yellowman's early-1980s breakout period.
- **Category:Dancehall** — most files are 2000s–2020s (e.g. **Street dance Kingston Jamaica
  passa passa.jpg**, dated 2007-09-13, photographer `nickolette`/Flickr, CC BY-SA 2.5 — a real
  Kingston street-dance photo, but the Passa Passa street-dance phenomenon it documents is a
  2000s revival, not 1980s dancehall).
- **Fishbone** (US ska) — one Commons file, **Fishbone-28.jpg**, dated 2008-05-02, photographer
  Auggie Tolosa (Flickr), CC BY-SA 2.0 — [source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fishbone-28.jpg). A 2008 club show, not the band's 1979–85 formation era.
- **The Toasters, The Untouchables** (NYC/LA ska) — no usable Commons category found; "Toasters"
  on Commons resolves to kitchen appliances, not the band.

**Assessment:** identical pattern to the skinhead subject. The professional photographers of
the actual 2 Tone/early-dancehall/early-US-ska scene (notably Syd Shelton, who shot Rock
Against Racism and 2 Tone package tours; and the agency/press photographers who covered
Yellowman's rise) retain conventional copyright; nothing from that circle is on Commons under
an open license. Only much-later fan concert photography of the surviving/reunited acts is
CC-licensed, and none of it reads as "vintage" imagery.

## 4. Notable photographers/archives whose work is NOT openly licensed (off-limits)

1. **Homer Sykes** — documentary photographer whose 1969–70 British skinhead and street-culture
   images are the most frequently reproduced record of the scene; held by his own agency/estate,
   conventional copyright, editorial licensing only.
2. **Syd Shelton** — Rock Against Racism and 2 Tone-era tour photographer (Specials, Selecter,
   Madness gigs, multiracial crowds); his archive (partly held by Autograph ABP / V&A) is
   rights-managed, not CC.
3. **Gavin Watson** ("Skins" / "Skins & Punks" photobooks) — retrospective photographic record
   of the British skinhead scene from the inside; published and licensed commercially through
   his own imprint and publishers (Damn/Trojan-adjacent books), not open-licensed.
4. **Adrian Boot** — prolific Jamaican-music photographer (Bob Marley, Studio One-era Kingston,
   Trojan Records artists); archive licensed through Urban Image/PYMCA and similar agencies,
   all-rights-reserved.
5. **Getty Images / Alamy / Shutterstock editorial archives** — hold by far the largest volume
   of studio, sound-system, and dance-hall imagery from this era (confirmed via search: Getty
   alone lists 100+ Desmond Dekker and 19,000+ "Kingston Jamaica" results), but all editorial,
   none redistributable in a software product.
6. **National Library of Jamaica digital collections** — extensive digitized photograph holdings
   exist (`nljdigital.nlj.gov.jm`), but the site states no clear open-reuse license for
   redistribution/modification in a product; treat as unclear/unusable until a rights inquiry
   confirms otherwise.

## Summary of what's missing (gaps)

- No open-licensed period photography exists for: Desmond Dekker, Prince Buster, Jimmy Cliff,
  The Skatalites, Toots and the Maytals, Byron Lee and the Dragonaires, Bob Marley and the
  Wailers, or any Kingston recording-studio interior/session, from the 1960s/70s.
- No open-licensed period photography exists for the 1969 multiracial skinhead scene at all —
  this is a hard gap, not a search-depth problem.
- No open-licensed period photography exists for 2 Tone bands, early Jamaican dancehall
  (Yellowman-era), or early American ska (Fishbone/Toasters/Untouchables) from their actual
  1979–85 era.
- The only genuinely period, genuinely on-license image set found across this entire brief is
  the two Millie Small Anefo frames (both CC BY-SA, both airport-arrival portraits rather than
  studio/dance-hall atmosphere).

**Recommendation:** given how sparse the openly-licensed record is, the login-page background
will likely need either (a) a licensed stock/archival purchase (Getty/Alamy/Adrian Boot's
agency) cleared specifically for redistribution in an open-source binary, (b) commissioning or
sourcing a public-domain-adjacent generic image (record shop, vinyl, sound-system speaker stack
as an object rather than a dated crowd photo) that doesn't require a named-era, named-person
photograph, or (c) accepting Millie Small's Anefo portrait (CC BY-SA, with attribution and
share-alike carried through to the binary's credits) as the one authentic period option.
