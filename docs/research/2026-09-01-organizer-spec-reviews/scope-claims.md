# Adversarial review (scope-claims), 2026-09-01, opus agent

I read the spec in full, plus ROADMAP.md, the conformance-corpus research, the plugin-adoption survey it rests on, the uploader spec's ratified journey addendum, the prod-polish plan, and STATUS.

# Adversarial review: 2026-09-01-dubplate-organizer-design.md

## Findings, most severe first

**[blocker] 1. Rung 3 is declared "the initiative's point of no return, taken only on rung 2's proof" — and "proof" is never defined, nor is there a rollback.**
The whole ladder hangs on a gate the spec does not specify: no conformance threshold (100% verdict agreement? 99%? distance-ranking agreement within epsilon?), no policy for known-acceptable divergences, no statement of what a single stratum-3 mismatch means. "Proven behaviorally equivalent before it replaces the subprocess" is asserted in the opening paragraph as a property of the plan rather than a measurable criterion. Worse, the only stated safety net is "the beets database is archived in R2, never deleted" — an archive is not a rollback, because after the swap the Go store diverges from that snapshot immediately. If the Go matcher misbehaves in production a week after rung 3, the spec has no procedure and no acceptance criteria to have failed.

**[blocker] 2. "Documented and versioned" client-agnostic API: no version scheme, no doc artifact, no owner, no rung — and the parity mechanism actively fights versioning.**
Line 52 promises the contract is "documented and versioned" without naming a versioning strategy (URL path? header? semver on what?), a compatibility policy, or where the document lives; no ladder rung names an API-documentation deliverable (rung 6's "admin API spine" is code, not contract docs). Meanwhile the parity guarantee — "one contract-test suite runs against both implementations... an operation added to one side fails the suite until the other side has it" — enforces *lockstep* between engine, server, and clients. Lockstep is the opposite of a versioned public contract: it means the wire types are whatever the Go interface currently is, and any third-party or mobile client breaks whenever the interface moves. The spec cannot have both "the types are... the API's wire types; one vocabulary, not two" and a stable versioned surface for clients it does not control.

**[blocker] 3. The same one API carries "session creation, tus upload, journey, history, config, admin verbs" with no authorization model stated.**
The auth seam is specified only as "the server verifies an identity assertion" — that is authentication. Nothing in the spec says how admin verbs (config, review-queue triage, doctor) are separated from upload verbs on a surface explicitly opened to "third-party web UIs, and native mobile apps" as equal clients. ROADMAP carries the answer ("admin = AdminEmail match, no role system") but the spec drops it, so the document that declares "the HTTP API is the product surface" says nothing about who may call half of it. For a spec whose central architectural claim is API-as-product, an unstated authz model is the load-bearing gap.

**[blocker] 4. Scope honesty: the spec designs for an audience its own out-of-scope wall says does not exist, and never states who the product is for.**
"Multi-tenancy is out: one library, one group is the product essence" sits 190 lines after a client roster of "third-party web UIs, and native mobile apps" served "as equals," plus versioning, capability discovery, machine-readable errors, and optional TS codegen. Who writes a third-party web UI against a single-family server, and what makes a native iOS app worth building for a group that fits in one household? The spec has no audience statement anywhere; it inherits ROADMAP's "family or friends" essence while funding a public-platform surface. Either the world-facing framing is real — in which case multi-tenancy, packaging, and distribution are the load-bearing work, not out of scope — or the roster is aspiration written as requirement.

**[major] 5. Packaging — the repo's own stated primary product goal — is resequenced behind all six rungs, and the spec does not acknowledge the cost.**
CLAUDE.md states "Ease of setup by a stranger is a primary product goal." ROADMAP resequences `packaging` after `dubplate-organizer` "so it templates the final two-binary shape rather than an intermediate." That is a defensible engineering argument and an indefensible product one: the single highest-leverage step toward the world-facing claims in section "Client-agnostic API" is now gated behind a full reimplementation of a working dependency. The spec carries the resequencing as a settled input and never argues it, so the reader cannot tell whether the tradeoff was weighed or inherited.

**[major] 6. "Each rung is shippable" is false for rungs 1 and 2 by the spec's own text, and the risk is not actually risk-ordered.**
Rung 1 says "No behavior change." Rung 2 is "behind the harness until conformance holds." Neither ships anything a user can see; the first user-visible change is rung 3, which is also the irreversible one. So "the ladder is risk-ordered; each rung is shippable" describes repo-internal milestones, not shippable increments, and the entire production risk of the initiative is concentrated in the single rung that the spec also calls the point of no return. The honest statement is the inverse of the one written: rungs 1–2 retire *estimation* risk, not deployment risk.

**[major] 7. The plugin evidence is restated as fact with its source's caveat deleted, and the "28–90% band" is not a band.**
The spec says "the empirically top beets plugins." The survey it rests on (n=87 GitHub-published configs, 84 repos) states its own limit plainly: "Sampling bias: dotfiles publishers are power users... absolute counts overstate the casual user; the RANKING... is the durable signal." The spec keeps the numbers' authority and drops the caveat. Worse, the claimed 28–90% band contains five plugins the spec does not ship — info (40.2%), lyrics (35.6%), edit (31.0%), missing (31.0%), fromfilename (29.9%) — and silently drops `mbsync`/MusicBrainz sync, which the survey's own "ship in the box" conclusion includes. The band is a post-hoc description of a chosen list, not a selection criterion.

**[major] 8. "Scrapers and the sub-10% beets plugin tail stay refused" conflates two disjoint sets, and the survey contradicts it.**
The out-of-scope line reads as one category. In the survey, `lyrics` — the canonical scraper, and the source doc's own example ("scrapers (lyrics sites)") — sits at 35.6%, tied for fifth. So the refusal of scrapers is a policy judgment about API fragility that the spec disguises as a popularity threshold. Say which it is: a maintenance-risk refusal (defensible, and it should be stated as such) or an adoption-threshold refusal (which the data does not support).

**[major] 9. Quarterly re-capture and pinned-beets-as-oracle are permanent operational commitments with no owner, no automation, and no remediation policy.**
"Freshness comes from re-capturing a rotating subset quarterly and on beets version bumps" assigns recurring work to nobody, in a single-maintainer repo, with no timer, no CI job, and no failure mode described. It also silently makes pinned beets a permanent development dependency long after rung 3 removes it from production — which contradicts the reader's natural inference from "the estate keeps running beets until rung 3 completes." Most importantly: when a post-swap quarterly run finds a divergence, what happens? The harness is defined as a pre-swap gate only; after the swap it produces findings with no defined response.

**[major] 10. "Un-idiomatic code adjacent to a change is in scope for that change" codifies the exact failure mode the workstation's pass-sizing rule names.**
CLAUDE.md defines "accretion by adjacency" as a named defect: "work joins a task because it sits next to it, each addition defensible alone and none weighed against the total." The spec elevates that to policy. Combined with "A rung that ships working but unclean has not shipped," every rung acquires an unbounded, unestimatable cleanup surface, and the completion criterion is a subjective judgment the same context that wrote the code will make. Bound it — adjacent cleanup within the touched file, or a filed follow-up — or it will silently double rung sizes.

**[major] 11. "Beauty and cleanliness as architecture" is half enforceable and half vibes, and the spec does not distinguish them.**
The enforceable half is real and named: go-conventions, elm-conventions, bubbletea-design, tui-visual-verify, the DaisyUI/runes mandate. The unenforceable half — "small single-purpose packages with narrow seams," "beauty," "adapted rather than reinvented," "shipped clean" — has no gate that could fail. Ask what a check would actually assert: package count? exported-symbol count per package? there is no proposal. Also, the visual-fidelity method the repo already gates on requires reference *screenshots* before any plan; the spec names poplar as a reference repo, not a capture set, and asserts without evidence that an email client's mail-list idiom transfers to a library browser and an import wizard.

**[major] 12. TUSKit and tus-android-client are called "mature" with no citation, and "first-class consumers, not ports" does not follow from library existence.**
No version, no last-release date, no maintenance signal, no research doc — unlike the MusicBrainz corpus work, which is sourced line by line. The register here is confident where the evidence is absent. And the inference is weak on its own terms: upload transport is the easy part of a mobile client. The hard parts are exactly the ones the spec names elsewhere and does not resolve for mobile — non-browser token acquisition, background upload continuation on iOS, and the fact that the reference auth implementation is Cloudflare Access, whose service tokens are org-admin-issued credentials a stranger does not have.

**[major] 13. Renaming the live production binary to `dubplate-server` is one clause in rung 5, with no migration cost accounted anywhere.**
"`dubplate-server` is today's estate binary renamed" touches every systemd unit, the deploy script, `/etc/dubplate`, `/opt/dubplate`, the pings check slugs, the runbook, and STATUS — the same surface the musicbox→dubplate rename just finished traversing at go-rewrite. It also creates a name collision: the *local* binary keeps the name `dubplate`, so installing the organizer on the box shadows the server's historical name and its config directory. The spec should either fund the rename as a rung-5 deliverable with a box-write plan, or keep `dubplate` as the server and name the local binary something else.

**[major] 14. "The server is fully functional with no streamer configured" contradicts the uploader's ratified terminal state, and invents a journey state outside the settled vocabulary.**
The uploader spec's ratified addendum fixes the vocabulary as "uploading, checking, matching, importing, in-library, pushed-back, queued, stalled," with "'in your library' appears when the link is real." This spec introduces "imported, scan pending" as the degraded terminal state without saying it amends that contract. And "fully functional" overstates: with no streamer, the product's entire stated payoff — the album playable in a library — never arrives. Call it what it is (the organizer functions; the journey does not complete) and file the vocabulary amendment against the uploader spec.

**[major] 15. "Navidrome, Jellyfin, and generic Subsonic are equal peers" is unverified on the one capability the seam is built around.**
The seam's verbs are "scan trigger, scan status, album deep link, capability discovery." `startScan`/`getScanStatus` are not in the classic Subsonic API — they are an OpenSubsonic/Navidrome-side extension — so "generic Subsonic" is very likely a degraded peer, not an equal one, and the spec's own "capability discovery" clause exists precisely because they are not equal. Verify against the OpenSubsonic extension list and the Jellyfin library-refresh API before rung 6, and downgrade the wording to what the APIs actually support.

**[major] 16. "Review queue" carries three different meanings across the estate's specs, and this spec adds the third without noticing.**
The uploader's ratified addendum ABOLISHES the review path on the uploader surface, keeping "the timer-driven inbox path... as the admin-only side door." STATUS still carries two albums in a filesystem review directory from the seed. This spec then names the review queue as a server-owned concern, makes "review-queue triage... the first remote job," and separately defines it as the remote *representation of interactive ask-resolution* — a fourth thing again. Define the term once, or the ops verb will be built against the wrong one.

**[major] 17. Publish-only "sync" does not say what happens when the server disagrees with the local verdict.**
"The local organizer publishes a finished album to a server through the same validated intake API as every other client, so the server's custody guarantees hold." That means the server re-runs matching on an album the local engine already matched and tagged — same engine, different config, different collection routing, possibly a different MusicBrainz snapshot. When local said confident-match and the server pushes back, the user has a finished album the system refuses. Not addressed; and the redone work is presented as a benefit rather than a cost.

**[major] 18. No sizing anywhere, after two consecutive ceiling overruns.**
Six rungs, each getting "its own spec and plan when its turn comes," with no token ceiling, no pass count, no rung-level estimate, and no statement of which rungs are one pass versus several. The go-rewrite spent ≈5.0M of 5.5M; the uploader is currently at ≈6.2M against a 5M ceiling with Pass B unfinished. A spec that sets build order for the largest initiative in the repo and offers no magnitude is asking for the same overrun a third time. Even a crude per-rung band would change how rungs 4–6 are scoped.

**[minor] 19. "beets has no HTTP-cassette infrastructure, so the project owns its own minimal cassette format" is a non sequitur, against the spec's own research.**
The research doc it cites says plainly: "Go has mature VCR-style tooling (dnaeon/go-vcr, seborama/govcr)." Whether beets records cassettes has no bearing on whether an existing Go library can. This is a build-versus-adopt decision presented as a forced consequence; state the actual reason (fixture shape control, determinism, no dependency) or adopt go-vcr.

**[minor] 20. Three config philosophies coexist with no arbiter.**
`dubplate-server` keeps "the full required-no-defaults wall"; `dubplate` local's "config philosophy is minimal with sane defaults"; integrations "get explicit enable flags" — and an enable flag is a default (off). They share one engine and one ops vocabulary, so engine-level settings (path formats, collection routing, match thresholds) must resolve differently depending on which binary loaded them, which the spec never says. CLAUDE.md's rule — "no site-specific value, path, or identity default outside `internal/config`" — needs an explicit reconciliation here.

**[minor] 21. Rungs 4 and 5 cannot overlap as freely as claimed, and overlapping violates the one-executor rule.**
The local TUI (rung 5) is specified to "triage duplicates, run hygiene tools" — capabilities rung 4 builds. So rung 5's surface consumes rung 4's output, and "Rungs 5 and 6 touch different surfaces than rung 4" is contradicted by the spec's own TUI feature list. Separately, overlapping rungs means two executors in one repo, which the workstation rule forbids without explicit coordination; if overlap is intended, name the worktree strategy.

**[minor] 22. Capability discovery names two unrelated, unspecified protocols.**
"capability discovery" appears in the streamer seam (what a streamer server supports) and again in the ops seam ("the interface reports capabilities... the TUI renders what is available"). Neither is specified, neither is owned by a rung, and reusing one term for two mechanisms will produce one confused implementation.

**[minor] 23. Putting `friendly_reason` copy in the wire payload makes copy edits API changes.**
"The copy contract's `friendly_reason` strings travel in payloads so every client renders the same kind rejection language" is the right call for consistency and has an unstated consequence: once the API is versioned and third parties consume it, human-readable copy is part of the versioned contract, and there is no localization stance. Say whether clients may override, and whether a copy revision bumps the contract.

**[minor] 24. The "Roadmap revisions this spec carries" list is incomplete.**
It names three revisions and omits at least two the spec makes: ROADMAP's "migration between rungs is backup/restore" is superseded by publish-only intake, and ROADMAP's "Access-authenticated via the cloudflared access token flow" is generalized into a verify-identity seam with Access as merely reference. It also does not touch ROADMAP's product-essence line ("a shared Navidrome server") or CLAUDE.md's identical framing, both of which the Navidrome demotion contradicts.

**[minor] 25. Store claims presented as settled with no evidence.**
"SQLite through the pure-Go modernc driver" carries no performance, concurrency, or library-size envelope — probably fine at family scale, but nothing states the scale it must hold. "The relational model beets proved out survives" treats beets' schema as validated design; beets' flexible-attribute key-value tables are idiosyncratic, and the spec neither evaluates nor commits to reproducing them. "The single-file snapshot backup stays identical" is true of the mechanism and false of the contents.

**[minor] 26. Corpus sizing and stratum ratios are one agent's estimate presented as design.**
"Roughly 150 to 300 cases," 40/50/10, appear identically in the research doc with no basis stated for why 150 is sufficient to establish equivalence for a matcher whose distance model has dozens of penalty terms. The stratum-1 work — hand-porting 60–120 inline Python test shapes into Go fixtures and deriving verdicts by running real beets — is the largest manual labor in rung 1 and its volume is never named.

## The three I would fix first

1. **Define rung 3's gate and its rollback** (finding 1). The irreversible step currently has an undefined entry criterion and no exit. Write the conformance threshold, the divergence-exception policy, and the procedure for reverting production to beets after the swap — before rung 1's plan is written, because the threshold determines what the corpus must contain.
2. **Resolve the API's audience, then fund or delete the world-facing surface** (findings 2, 3, 4). Either state the audience as one group, drop "versioned"/third-party/mobile to "nothing precludes it later," and delete the unfunded protocol work; or keep the public claim and add explicit rung-owned deliverables for the contract document, the versioning and compatibility policy, and the authorization model — plus an argument for why lockstep contract tests are compatible with client-agnostic versioning.
3. **Restate the plugin selection on its real basis, and bound the cleanliness mandate** (findings 7, 8, 10). Rewrite the batteries scope as a maintenance-risk judgment carrying the survey's power-user caveat, and reconcile the missing `mbsync` and the in-band exclusions. In the same edit, bound "un-idiomatic adjacent code is in scope" to a stated radius so it stops being an open-ended scope grant on every rung.
