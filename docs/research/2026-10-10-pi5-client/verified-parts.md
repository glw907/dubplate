# Build the Pi 5 stereo client on a Waveshare 13.3-inch display

Build around the Waveshare 13.3-inch DSI touch display (about $153 to $157) powered from its own USB-C port, a Pimoroni NVMe Base under the Pi with a 1 TB SSD, and a HiFiBerry DAC2 HD ($109) on the GPIO header for analog RCA out, with the Pi on the official 27 W supply. Run Raspberry Pi OS Trixie with PipeWire, Feishin, a local Navidrome over an rclone-synced FLAC folder, and raspotify. The parts come to roughly **$525 to $555 excluding the Pi** (about $50 more if you buy the display on Amazon), well under the $800 budget, and I recommend leaving the remainder unspent because nothing further is audibly or visibly better. Evidence is thinnest for the display: Waveshare documents Pi 5, Bookworm, Trixie and labwc, and one Raspberry Pi engineer's forum test shows the panel running at 59.94 fps on a Pi 5, but **no independent Pi 5 touch report exists, so you are close to its first test case** and should buy inside a return window. The official 10-inch Touch Display 2 ($80, grade A) stays the named fallback. The panel's mounting is now settled from Waveshare's drawing: the metal-back version has 100 x 100 VESA holes, and the assembly weighs about 1.1 to 1.3 kg, so it goes on a spring-free friction arm with 50 mm standoffs to clear the Pi. The follow-up research closed the other open questions by design rather than by test. The display has its own 5 V USB-C power port, so it never needs the GPIO header. The NVMe board moves under the Pi, which leaves the header to the DAC alone and removes the unproven HAT-on-HAT stack. One risk survives: touch may fail to come back after the screen blanks, a known fault on the official display under labwc. The software section works around it by dimming the backlight instead of blanking the output.

The display deserves the plainest statement. Your ruling stands: the 13.3-inch is the build, and the first failure mode is simply that it does not behave as the wiki says.

### The two discrepancies in the display notes

The wiki gives `dtoverlay=vc4-kms-dsi-waveshare-panel,13_3_inch_4lane` for Pi 5, while a Kiwi Electronics listing quotes `13_3_inch_2lane` and says touch is one-point without Waveshare's driver ([Kiwi Electronics](https://www.kiwi-electronics.com/en/raspberry-pi-boards-cases-addons-and-accessories-59/displays-monitors-for-the-raspberry-pi-413/13-3-inch-dsi-touch-display-for-raspberry-pi-20565)). I cannot settle this from the sources, so treat my reading as an inference. The Pi 5 exposes four DSI lanes, and the official 10-inch display also requires 4-lane DSI on Pi 5 ([Raspberry Pi product page](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/touch-display-2/)); the 2-lane variant most plausibly serves older boards with two-lane ports, and the retailer text may describe an older revision. Start with `4lane`, the vendor's Pi 5 line. If the panel stays black, try `2lane` before declaring the unit dead. The one-point claim has a likelier explanation as well: the wiki's default Mouse Emulation mode reports one pointer. Count touch slots with `libinput debug-events` after switching to Multitouch; if the panel really reports one point under a current kernel, that is a return, not a tuning problem.

## The Pi rides on the display's back, and that decides the mount

A DSI panel is tied to the Pi by a short ribbon, so the NVMe Base, the Pi and the DAC HAT form a stack on the display's rear. That has five consequences.

First, weight and holes. Buy the version with the full metal back panel (Amazon B0DP2SDYN6, or direct from Waveshare); the "(B)" version without a back panel has no VESA holes. Waveshare's dimension drawing for the metal-back version shows a **100 x 100 mm VESA pattern with M4 holes**, an outline of 324.6 x 203.0 mm, a depth of about 22 mm, an active area of 294.2 x 165.8 mm, and the Pi's 58 x 49 mm M2.5 holes in the middle of the back ([Waveshare product page](https://www.waveshare.com/13.3inch-DSI-LCD.htm), drawing "details-size"). Neither Waveshare nor Amazon publishes a net weight. OpenElab gives a **1.341 kg shipping weight "according to the official package-content listing"** (its store data says 1,300 g), which includes the box, foam and cables ([OpenElab](https://openelab.io/products/waveshare-13-3inch-high-resolution-dsi-capacitive-touch-display-1920x1080)). The bare panel is therefore about 1 kg, and with the Pi, cooler, NVMe Base, SSD and DAC (about 150 g together) the assembly lands near **1.1 to 1.3 kg** (my estimate from those figures; weigh it on arrival). That is below the documented minimum load of every spring or gas arm found: Ergotron LX and Humanscale M2.1 about 2.3 kg, Brateck and Amazon Basics about 2 kg ([Work While Walking](https://www.workwhilewalking.com/humanscale-m2-monitor-arm-review), [Tech for Good](https://techforgood.com.au/products/brateck-economy-single-screen-spring-assisted-monitor-arm-fit-most-17-32-monitor-up-to-9-kg-vesa-75x75-100x100)). A spring tuned for a heavier screen lifts a light one, so the screen would drift upward and bounce when poked. The Ergotron LX Pro is the only candidate with a possibly lower floor (one listing says 1.8 kg), but the figures conflict between listings and I could not reach Ergotron's manual ([Inet.se](https://www.inet.se/produkt/2302437/ergotron-lx-pro-desk-monitor-arm)).

Second, rigidity. No source tested touch-induced wobble on any arm. My inference is that the spring is the drift source, so a friction-locked or fixed mount outperforms a spring arm at this weight, and a short, thick, low-reach mount shortens the lever that every tap acts on.

Third, the mount. At 1.1 to 1.3 kg, buy a friction arm with no spring: nothing pushes up, so a light display cannot drift, and the joints lock with screws. The pick is the **Amazon Basics single monitor stand, the steel pole-and-arm model** (about $30, 13 to 30 inch screens, up to 22 lb, VESA 75/100 with a detachable plate, C-clamp for surfaces 0.8 to 3.9 in thick, height 7.9 to 14.2 in on the pole, and more than 18,000 Amazon ratings at 4.3 stars) ([Newegg](https://www.newegg.com/p/0RC-07NF-00037)). The **VIVO STAND-V001** is the same design at about $35 (22 lb, 13 to 32 inch, joints that tighten) ([Black Box Safety](https://blackboxsafety.com/products/vivo-stand-v001)). Neither lists a minimum weight, which fits a design with no spring. A pole arm sets height once on the pole collar and swings and tilts on friction joints, which suits a screen that is poked rather than moved often. The portable-monitor arms rated for light screens top out too close to this weight: KUUVAN's 13 to 18.5 inch clamp arm is rated to 3.3 lb (1.5 kg) and has four reviews ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Portable-Monitor-Holder-13-18-5-Screens/dp/B0HFJ32Y67)). The gas arms stay ruled out, and the LX Pro's minimum is still unpublished.

Fourth, clearance. The Pi's holes sit inside the 100 x 100 VESA square, so an arm's VESA plate bolted flat would hit the stack. The NVMe Base and SSD under the Pi, the Pi with its Active Cooler, and the DAC raised on booster standoffs with its RCA jacks rise roughly 55 to 60 mm off the back (my estimate; measure the assembled stack). Space the arm's plate off the panel with four **M4 hex standoffs about 65 mm long** and M4 screws (generic hardware, a few dollars), or whatever length clears the measured stack by a few millimetres. Commercial VESA spacer kits stop at 18 to 30 mm ([3idee spacer](https://www.saturn.de/de/product/_3idee-spc-100-vesa-adapter-schwarz-141885467.html)), so buy plain standoffs. Leave a side open for the ribbon and the cables. The other route is to move the Pi onto the arm or shelf with a longer Pi 5 display cable, but a long DSI run is outside what Waveshare documents, so I would not start there.

The older candidates remain as fallbacks: the VIVO V101N (13 to 32 inch, VESA 75/100, 17.6 lb maximum, clamp to 3.25 in, minimum not stated) and VIVO mechanical tablet arms (5.5 lb capacity with adapter) ([Newegg Business](https://www.neweggbusiness.com/p/9B-15Z-00A5-008K9), [PC Liquidations](https://www.pcliquidations.com/p152705-vivo-tablet-holder-and)). For a cabinet top with no clamp edge, a fixed RAM D11 pedestal with a 75 mm VESA pattern runs about EUR 48 ([Barcode Site](https://www.barcodesite.eu/ram-mounts-d11-series-rap-d11-7-v75.html)). The Ergotron Neo-Flex Tablet Arm fits best on paper, with 18 lb capacity, no listed minimum and 75/100 holes, but it is discontinued ([Levata](https://www.levata.com/ergotron-45-306-101-rack)). If the official 10-inch fallback ever replaces the 13.3-inch, it has no VESA holes and needs a printed adapter like CNX's (drilled holes, 35 mm M2.5 screws) ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/07/22/raspberry-pi-touch-display-2-10%E2%80%B3-portrait-review-3d-printed-vesa-mount/)).

Fifth, cables. Everything that leaves the stack hangs on the moving part: the Pi's 27 W lead, the display's own USB-C power lead, the RCA pair to the amp, the keyboard and mouse dongle or cable, and Ethernet during the first sync. A stiff bundle will pull a light mount out of position, so run it along the arm with slack at each joint, and use a right-angle RCA adapter or a short pigtail so the jacks do not stick out sideways from the back. Keep the RCA run as short as the amp allows. Line-level hum is the only audio risk from length, and the earlier report's ground-loop isolator (about $15 est.) is the cure if it appears. For airflow, the Active Cooler's fan stays off at light load in practice (idle about 45 C with the cooler) ([Raspberry Pi news](https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/heating-and-cooling-raspberry-pi-5/)), but the stack sits against the panel's back, so leave an air gap and check the case temperature during a library scan.

## Audio and storage: the strongest configuration and what it risks

The first draft of this report stacked the official M.2 HAT+ on the Pi and the DAC on top of it. The follow-up research found that layout sound electrically and weak mechanically. Electrically there is no conflict. The M.2 HAT+ schematic labels its header "Stackable HAT+" and shows it using only power and the ID pins, with data over the PCIe ribbon ([M.2 HAT+ schematic](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/m2-hat-plus/raspberry-pi-m2-hat-plus-schematics.pdf)). The DAC2 HD uses the I2S pins (GPIO 18 to 21), I2C (GPIO 2 and 3) and GPIO 16 ([HiFiBerry GPIO usage](https://www.hifiberry.com/docs/hardware/gpio-usage-of-hifiberry-boards/)). The Raspberry Pi HAT+ specification explicitly allows one legacy HAT beside one Stackable HAT+, told apart by EEPROM address ([HAT+ specification](https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/1215-raspberry-pi-hat/documents/RP-008281-DS-1-hat-plus-specification.pdf)). The mechanics fail, though. The M.2 HAT+'s 16 mm header is female on top and too short to carry a HAT, and forum threads from 2024 and 2025 found no working fix once the Active Cooler is fitted ([Blokas forum](https://community.blokas.io/t/pisound-raspberry-pi-5-active-cooler-pcie-m-2-hat/5405), [SparkFun forum](https://community.sparkfun.com/t/stacking-a-sparkfun-qwiic-phat-with-a-m-2-hat-on-a-raspberry-pi-5/64302)). Nobody reports a working DAC-on-top build with the official board.

So put the NVMe under the Pi. The **Pimoroni NVMe Base** mounts beneath the board, connects over the PCIe ribbon, takes 2230, 2242 and 2280 drives, and ships 20 mm pass-through bolts for a HAT above; the MagPi review says it "doesn't obstruct a cooling fan, nor tie up the GPIO pins" ([Pimoroni](https://shop.pimoroni.com/en-us/products/nvme-base), [MagPi](https://magpi.raspberrypi.com/articles/nvme-base-review)). moOde's founder recommended it for exactly this DAC-plus-NVMe case in the earlier forum thread ([moOde forum](https://moodeaudio.org/forum/showthread.php?pid=65993)). It also removes the second HAT EEPROM from the ID pins, and the 2280 slot lifts the 1 TB ceiling of the 2230 Raspberry Pi SSD. One user found 25 mm standoffs strained its ribbon and 20 mm worked, so use the bolts it ships with. Pimoroni says WD SN350 and SN570 drives fail to boot on it; the Raspberry Pi SSD is the safe 1 TB pick, and a 2 TB 2280 drive is the step up if the library needs room.

The DAC then sits on the Pi's own header, and the only obstacle left is the Active Cooler, which stands 13.7 mm tall ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20231101-raspberry-pi-5-active-cooler)). Pimoroni sells a booster header and extended M2.5 standoffs for exactly this; HATs made after the Pi 5 commonly ship 16 mm standoffs for the same reason ([Pimoroni HAT boosters](https://shop.pimoroni.com/en-us/products/extended-m2-5-standoffs-for-pi-hats)). This is the most common HAT arrangement on a cooled Pi 5, so it is the well-trodden path.

For the DAC I pick the **HiFiBerry DAC2 HD** over the Raspberry Pi DAC+ or DAC Pro. Archimago measured it at **2.04 Vrms from the RCA jacks, better than -90 dB and under 0.003% THD+N, with low noise and low jitter**, and found it an objective improvement over the DAC+ Pro generation ([Archimago](https://archimago.blogspot.com/2021/02/measurements-hifiberry-dac2-hd-hat-for.html)). That same measurement used Pi 3 and 4, not Pi 5; Archimago found the host Pi did not matter, but I2S timing on Pi 5 is untested by him. HiFiBerry acknowledged that some of its drivers broke on Pi 5 and fixed them in Linux 6.1.77, naming the DAC+ Standard, Amp2 and DAC+ ADC but not the DAC2 HD ([HiFiBerry blog](https://www.hifiberry.com/blog/changes-in-hifiberry-drivers/)). Its shop page says "all Pi's with 40pin GPIO", with Pi 5 unnamed. The only Pi 5 report is a May 2024 Core Electronics forum reply saying "it looks like it does work" with "slight compatibility issues with the overlays" ([Core Electronics forum](https://forum.core-electronics.com.au/t/hifiberry-dac2-hd-ce08660/20011)); those overlay issues predate the 6.1.77 fixes. That is why I grade it B, not A. The Pi DAC Pro and DAC+ are first-party, powered from the header, 0-2 V RMS and in production until at least January 2028, but Raspberry Pi's pages do not name Pi 5 and I found no independent measurement of either ([Raspberry Pi DAC Pro](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/dac-pro/)). The DAC+ uses a PCM5122, and Archimago found the HiFiBerry DAC+ Pro with that chip family had the highest distortion of the DACs he tested, probably still inaudible but unmeasured here. The honest summary: the DAC2 HD buys measured quality, the Pi DAC buys first-party provenance, and neither is verified on Pi 5.

The residual risk in this layout is small and testable: no one has published this exact combination (NVMe Base, DAC2 HD, Active Cooler) on a Pi 5, and no source measures PCIe noise reaching an I2S DAC. The DAC2 HD has its own clock, which should make it less exposed (an inference). The only failure on record for any NVMe-plus-DAC Pi 5 was a weak power supply ([moOde forum](https://moodeaudio.org/forum/showthread.php?pid=65993)), which the official 27 W supply answers, and the display no longer draws from the Pi at all. The all-in-one 52Pi NVDAC (PCM5122, dual RCA, M.2 over a flat cable, about $23 to $30) is the cheaper integrated alternative, but it is grade C and its seller warns of NVMe compatibility problems ([52Pi wiki](https://wiki-md.52pi.com/docs/EP-0218)). A USB DAC is the other fallback, graded C: vendors claim Linux support but I found no confirmed positive Pi 5 report for any model, and one Pi 5 owner saw over-current faults with a Benchmark DAC3 ([Volumio forum](https://community.volumio.com/t/raspberrypi-thinks-my-benchmark-dac-is-an-adafruit-amp/70953?page=2)).

Display power no longer touches the header. Waveshare's wiki powers the panel through a 3-pin lead to the Pi's 5 V pins, and the DAC would cover those pins. Waveshare's own product images also label a USB-C connector on the panel's edge "5V Power Supply Port". It sits beside the 3-pin header ([Waveshare product image](https://www.waveshare.com/img/devkit/LCD/13.3inch-DSI-LCD/13.3inch-DSI-LCD-details-13.jpg)). Power the panel from its own USB-C supply. The DSI ribbon carries seven ground pins, so the panel and Pi share ground through it (an inference from the pinout in the same image). No text documents the panel's current draw or how the two power inputs combine, so use one input only, never both, and pick a supply of 2 A or more (my margin; a smaller sister board lists 0.5 A ([Waveshare 11.9inch wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/11.9inch_DSI_LCD))). Raspberry Pi documents no current limit for the GPIO 5 V pins. A separate supply keeps the backlight's load off them.

## Software: the earlier stack, updated for the new hardware

Install Raspberry Pi OS Trixie 64-bit with the default labwc desktop and flash the NVMe with Imager. With no SD card inserted, the Pi 5 boots from NVMe on any bootloader newer than December 2023 ([Raspberry Pi docs](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/m2-hat-plus.html), [Pimoroni](https://shop.pimoroni.com/en-us/products/nvme-base)). Keep the PCIe link at its default Gen 2; FLAC needs a tiny fraction of its 450 MB/s.

Three `config.txt` lines set up the hardware. The panel takes `dtoverlay=vc4-kms-dsi-waveshare-panel,13_3_inch_4lane` on the DSI1 port, and a Raspberry Pi engineer confirmed 4-lane at 59.94 fps on a Pi 5 in a forum test; 2-lane on the same panel ran at 33.67 Hz with shimmer, so the Kiwi listing's `2lane` line belongs to older boards ([Raspberry Pi forum](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=387866)). The DAC takes `dtoverlay=hifiberry-dacplushd` ([HiFiBerry DAC2 HD docs](https://www.hifiberry.com/docs/hardware/hifiberry-dac2-hd/)). Turn the onboard audio off by removing `dtparam=audio=on` or adding `noaudio` to the `vc4-kms-v3d` line. If the DAC's EEPROM read blocks the driver, `force_eeprom_read=0` is HiFiBerry's documented fallback. Confirm with `aplay -l` that the card appears.

Touch needs one setting. In labwc the touch entry ships as mouse emulation, which turns every touch into a click; set `mouseEmulation="no"` in `~/.config/labwc/rc.xml` or use the Control Centre toggle (Mouse Emulation versus Multitouch), which Raspberry Pi added for its own display and Waveshare documents for this one ([CNX tips](https://cnx-software.com/2025/03/12/tips-to-use-a-touchscreen-display-with-raspberry-pi-os-in-2025/?amp=1), [Waveshare wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/13.3inch_DSI_LCD)). Use squeekboard for the on-screen keyboard. Rotation in the GUI rotates touch too.

Screen sleep needs care, because blanking is where Pi touchscreens fail. On the official Touch Display 2 under labwc, a Home Assistant kiosk user blanking with swayidle and wlopm found about a quarter of wakes left touch dead until the app restarted ([Home Assistant community](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/kiosk-mode-for-raspberry-pi-with-touch-display/821196?page=9)). A Waveshare panel on a CM4 failed to come back after `wlr-randr --off`, while a Pi 5 on kernel 6.6 recovered ([Raspberry Pi forum](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=387866)). Nobody has tested this panel's wake, so design around it. Turn off output blanking in the Control Centre, and dim instead by writing 0 to the panel's sysfs backlight (Waveshare documents a 0 to 255 range in the wiki). A small idle script can set it to 0 after a few minutes and restore it on the next touch. The DSI output and the touch controller stay powered the whole time, so there is no wake for touch to miss. The cost is the panel logic left running, a small draw by my estimate.

Everything else carries over from the earlier research. Run Feishin 1.17.0, which publishes arm64 builds and an mpv backend, pinned to a release ([Feishin release](https://github.com/jeffvli/feishin/releases/tag/v1.17.0)), with two profiles: dubplate and the local server. Run Navidrome 0.64.2 or the lighter gonic 0.22.0 on the Pi over the synced folder ([Navidrome releases](https://api.github.com/repos/navidrome/navidrome/releases), [gonic releases](https://api.github.com/repos/sentriz/gonic/releases)). Sync with `rclone sync` over SFTP from the server's FLAC store, which is bit-exact and resumable and needs a file-level path on the box, an owner decision on dubplate's side; the `download` endpoint serves one album per request and would mean thousands of calls. Do the first sync over Ethernet, since Pi 5 Wi-Fi tops out near 217 Mbps and a metal case costs about 20% ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/raspberry-pi-5-wi-fi-faster), [LinuxLinks](https://www.linuxlinks.com/sunfounder-pironman-5-review-raspberry-pi/5/)). Add raspotify 0.48.3 for Spotify Connect, with go-librespot 0.10.3 as the alternate, and expect lossy 320 kbps Vorbis from either ([raspotify releases](https://api.github.com/repos/dtcooper/raspotify/releases), [go-librespot](https://github.com/devgianlu/go-librespot)). For bit-perfect output, add a PipeWire drop-in setting `default.clock.allowed-rates` to the DAC's rates, keep player volume at 100% with the amp's knob setting level, and confirm the file's rate in `/proc/asound/card*/pcm*p/sub*/hw_params` ([pipewire.conf(5)](https://docs.pipewire.org/page_man_pipewire_conf_5.html)). Bit-perfect holds only while one source plays; PipeWire mixes concurrent streams. Whether Feishin exposes mpv's exclusive-output option is unverified. The DAC2 HD tops out at the rates its manual lists, so build the rate list from `aplay` output rather than copying the earlier example.

## Verify on arrival, in this order

The order puts the checks that can end the build ahead of the ones that only tune it. Do all of them inside the display's return window.

1. **Weigh the display and measure the stack.** Confirm the metal back with its 100 x 100 M4 holes arrived, weigh the panel (expect about 1 kg), and measure how far the assembled stack stands off the back so the VESA standoffs clear it with a few millimetres spare.
2. **Boot the display alone.** Power it from its own USB-C supply, connect it to the Pi's DSI1 port with the supplied ribbon, add the `4lane` overlay, and confirm a picture. If it fails, return it and buy the official 10-inch.
3. **Count touch points.** Run `libinput debug-events`, switch to Multitouch, and put down several fingers. Confirm more than one slot registers. Then check scrolling and drag in Feishin.
4. **Backlight dimming.** Write 0 and then 255 to the panel's sysfs backlight and confirm touch still registers while dark. This is the sleep path the build relies on. As a bonus check, try `wlr-randr --off` and `--on` too; if touch survives that as well, output blanking is safe on this panel.
5. **Stack boot test.** Fit the NVMe Base and SSD under the Pi and the DAC on booster standoffs above, power the Pi from the 27 W supply, boot from NVMe, and read `aplay -l`. If it fails, test with the DAC removed.
6. **Audio level and hum.** Play silence at working volume with the DAC at fixed output; if you hear hum, add a passive ground-loop isolator.
7. **Bit-perfect rates.** Play 44.1, 96 and 192 kHz files and read `hw_params` for each. Check `dmesg` for over-current or underrun messages during 192 kHz playback.
8. **Feishin, Navidrome, raspotify.** Confirm touch scrolling, gapless playback, both server profiles, and that raspotify appears in Spotify and shares the DAC without a fight.
9. **Thermals.** Watch the SoC temperature through a Navidrome scan with the stack against the panel back.

## Conclusion

With a DSI panel, the display choice is also the mounting, cabling and heat decision. The follow-up research turned the two biggest open risks into design choices. The display takes its own USB-C power, and the NVMe board goes under the Pi, so nothing competes for the header. What remains is one untested combination of well-documented parts and one known touchscreen fault that the backlight-dimming setup sidesteps. A 1.1 to 1.3 kg assembly sits below every spring arm's minimum, and a $30 friction pole arm with long standoffs suits it better than a $200 gas arm. Money is not the constraint. The build lands near $525 to $555; keep the remainder for failure cover (the $80 official display, a spare DAC) rather than for gains that show only on a spec sheet.

The fallback ladder is clear. If the 13.3-inch fails on image or touch, buy the official Touch Display 2 10-inch, which has CNX's confirmation of ten-point touch and the on-screen keyboard but no VESA holes and a 480 g weight ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/07/22/raspberry-pi-touch-display-2-10%E2%80%B3-portrait-review-3d-printed-vesa-mount/)). Beware one catch: OMG! Ubuntu reports the 10-inch will not work with a Pi 5 stacked with an Active Cooler or M.2 HAT+, citing power and port limits ([OMG! Ubuntu](https://www.omgubuntu.co.uk/2026/07/raspberry-pi-10-inch-touch-display)). The NVMe Base layout already avoids the M.2 HAT+, but check that report against the Active Cooler before switching. If the DAC fails on the Pi 5, the DAC Pro, the 52Pi NVDAC and a USB DAC form a ladder from most to least first-party, each with its own thinner evidence.
