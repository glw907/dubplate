# Photo research: East/SE Asia, Continental Europe, Pacific (1940s-1980s)

Research log for dubplate login-page silver-print curation. Deep cuts, at most one
image per headline artist, license verified from source metadata (never inferred).

Method: Wikimedia Commons API (categorymembers + search), Europeana API, LOC API,
targeted web search. User-Agent sent, 1.5s+ sleep between calls.

Status: IN PROGRESS — appended after each region so an interruption loses nothing.

**Owner priority ruling (received mid-task):** core rotation weighting is
reggae/dub/dancehall/ska + punk/protopunk first; within this brief's regions,
European reggae/dub/ska and European punk (Dutch/German/Nordic/French/Eastern
Bloc 1977-85, plus Anefo/Bundesarchiv touring-act coverage) rank above the
other scenes and were searched first below, followed by Japanese punk/reggae,
then the rest of the brief in its original order.

## PRIORITY: European punk / reggae / ska (searched first per owner ruling)

**European punk — Netherlands (Anefo/Nationaal Archief via Commons): STRONG VEIN.**
Anefo (Dutch national press-photo agency, now Nationaal Archief) covered youth
culture including punk as a news beat from 1977 on. All Anefo material is
CC0 (`Nationaal Archief` credit, `Auteursrechthebbende: Nationaal Archief`,
license tag `cc0`), full-resolution scans (2400-3700px), photographer named
per Commons `Artist` field.

1. **Sex Pistols live at Paradiso, Amsterdam, 6 Jan 1977** — photographer Koen
   Suyk/Anefo. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Engelse_Punk_Rockgroep_de_Sex_Pistols_in_Paradiso,_Bestanddeelnr_928-9661.jpg
   — License: **CC0** (Nationaal Archief, verified via extmetadata `License:cc0`).
   3676x2448px. Description: "Engelse 'Punk' Rockgroep de Sex Pistols in
   Paradiso." Fit: 5 (club/dance-floor scene, foundational punk act, exact
   period). NOTE: Sex Pistols is a headline act — if the curation already has
   a Sex Pistols image from elsewhere, treat as the one slot for that band.
2. **First Dutch Punk Festival, Paradiso, Amsterdam, 18 Feb 1978** — crowd/
   "punkers" scene, photographer Rob Bogaerts/Anefo.
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eerste_Nederlandse_Punkfestival_in_Paradiso_Amsterdam_punkers_tijdens_festiva,_Bestanddeelnr_929-5806.jpg
   (siblings: Bestanddeelnr 929-5807/5808/5809, same event, different crowd
   framings) — License: **CC0**, verified. 2445x3688px. Fit: 5 (deep cut:
   crowd/scene photo, not a named headline artist, exactly the "club/dance-floor"
   texture the brief wants, and place — Paradiso — recurring across dates).
3. **Parkpop festival, Zuiderpark, Den Haag — "punkers" crowd**, multiple
   Anefo frames (Bestanddeelnr 931-5793, 931-5795, 933-3663). Not yet pulled for
   exact date/license string but same Anefo/CC0 pattern; Parkpop ran from 1980.
   Fit: 4 (pending metadata confirmation — treat as candidate, not yet verified).
4. **"Doe wat" festival, Deventer, 22 June 1984 — group of punks**, Anefo,
   photographer Marcel Antonisse. Bestanddeelnr 933-0117/933-0118/933-0119.
   Same CC0 pattern (confirmed via snippet metadata showing "Auteursrechthebbende").
   Fit: 3-4 (later date, 1984, still inside the brief's window; street/protest-
   adjacent punk scene rather than a music venue).
5. Assorted single Anefo punk street-portrait frames also surfaced (Zuiderpark,
   Stadhuis Amsterdam punk-girl testimony 1980s) — lower fit (posed/news rather
   than scene), not pursued further.

**European punk — Germany (Bundesarchiv): EFFECTIVELY EMPTY for punk itself.**

  regardless of subject interest. Fit: 2 (resolution-capped).
- Direct searches for "reggae Bundesarchiv" and "punk Bundesarchiv" (beyond the
  Nina Hagen hit) returned no further relevant results.

**European reggae/dub/ska: WEAK — archives hold flyers/posters, not performance
photographs, for this exact ask.**
- Europeana query for "reggae" (open reusability, image type, 1975-1989)
  returns almost entirely event-announcement flyer/poster scans from the
  Regional Archive Nijmegen (venue: Doornroosje club), e.g. a 3 May 1981
  Creation Rebel announcement, a 1982 Communication (reggae) flyer — all
  **CC BY-SA 4.0**, but these are printed flyers, not photographs, so they
  do not fit the silver-print brief (no photographer, no captured scene).
  Not recommended as candidates; noted for completeness.
- Direct Commons searches for "Anefo" + Marley/UB40/ska/reggaeband/Third World/
  Toots all returned **zero hits** — Anefo's punk coverage (a domestic youth-
  news beat) did not extend to touring reggae acts under these search terms.
- Commons Category:Reggae is dominated by 2000s-2020s live photos (Burning
  Spear at Reggae Geel 2023, CC0; Chronixx at Glastonbury 2015, CC BY-SA 4.0,
  etc.) — wrong period, not pursued.
- **Finding: no period (1970s-80s) European reggae/dub/ska performance
  photograph with a verifiable open license was located in this pass.** This
  scene came back effectively empty against the sources this brief specifies;
  a further pass would need UK-specific archives (which are out of this
  brief's European geography) or direct outreach to European reggae archives/
  photographers, not the generalist APIs used here.

**Japanese punk and reggae/dub (Mute Beat): EMPTY.**
- Commons searches for "Mute Beat," and for named acts (The Stalin, Friction,
  G.I.S.M.) returned no relevant photograph hits (only unrelated CIA-declassified-
  document PDFs matched "record group" on the second query).
- A web search for Japanese punk band photographs from 1979-1980 under CC/PD
  terms confirmed the gap: no Commons galleries or comprehensive open-licensed
  photo documentation of the Japanese punk scene (Aunt Sally, INU, The Stalin,
  Friction, G.I.S.M.) was found. This scene is a documented empty for the
  sources available; period Japanese punk photography largely sits with
  Japanese photographers/magazines (Shashin era, Watanabe Katsumi-adjacent
  circles) under Japanese copyright, not yet expired, and not on Commons.

**Conclusion for this region: the brief's named East/Southeast Asian scenes
are almost entirely absent from Wikimedia Commons, Europeana, and the LOC
API under the search terms tried.** This tracks the known bias of these
three sources toward North American, Western European, and (via Anefo/
Bundesarchiv) Dutch/German institutional photography. Getting real coverage
of jazz kissa, Group Sounds, Cantopop, luk thung, dangdut, etc. would need
Japanese/Korean/Southeast Asian national archives, broadcaster photo morgues,
or specialist collector communities/blogs — outside the four sources this
brief specifies, and not attempted here given the time box.
