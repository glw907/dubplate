## Reggae / ska / 2 Tone / British Caribbean

### Beth Lesser — HIGH
- **What she shot:** Jamaican dancehall culture in the early-to-mid 1980s (King Jammy's/King Tubby's studio scene, sound-system dances, the digital-riddim transition). ([reggae-vibes.com](https://www.reggae-vibes.com/beth-lesser-dancehall-the-rise-of-jamaican-dancehall-culture/), [reggae-vibes.com](https://www.reggae-vibes.com/beth-lesser-witness-to-reggae-photography/))
- **Living/estate:** Living; based in Toronto; still active in reggae journalism/photography circles.
- **Open licensing record:** Her photography archive is published under a **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives 4.0 International License** — a documented, already-public open-licensing stance. ([search summary, no single canonical URL surfaced in this pass — verify the exact hosting page, e.g. reggae-vibes.com or her own site, before quoting the exact CC variant in an outreach email](https://www.reggae-vibes.com/beth-lesser-witness-to-reggae-photography/))
- **Agency entanglement:** None found; small-press publishing history (Black Star, ECW Press, Soul Jazz) rather than a stock agency.
- **Public contact route:** No single official site surfaced in this pass; her publisher (Soul Jazz Records, ECW Press) or reggae-vibes.com (which regularly runs her writing) are plausible intermediaries. Verify a direct contact before drafting.
- **Reason for rank:** She already licenses CC-NC-ND; asking her to extend that to CC BY 4.0 for a handful of named images is a small, well-precedented ask, not a cold pitch to open a closed archive.

### Bishopsgate Institute — MEDIUM (institution)
- **What it holds:** Over 1,000,000 images (physical/digital) spanning London photography, protest movements, and — relevant here — likely Rock Against Racism/2 Tone-adjacent ephemera and photography, plus its large LGBTQ+ (Rebel Dykes) and labour-history collections. ([bishopsgate.org.uk](https://www.bishopsgate.org.uk/collections/photographic-collections/))
- **Who runs it:** Bishopsgate Institute, an independent London cultural/educational charity, since 1895.
- **Reuse/licensing stance:** Requires a formal **"Application for non-commercial purposes"** form, capped at 10 items per scan request, plus a permissions form; commercial/reproduction use likely requires a separate negotiated license, and copyright often still sits with the original photographer/donor rather than the Institute itself. ([bishopsgate.org.uk](https://www.bishopsgate.org.uk/archives/visit/))
- **Community/open project record:** Not confirmed in this pass; its scale and educational charity status suggest openness in principle, but no explicit CC or open-data statement was found.
- **Public contact route:** bishopsgate.org.uk/archives (visit/enquiry pages).
- **Reason for rank:** Good depth and mission fit for UK protest-culture imagery, but the process is bureaucratic and per-item copyright clearance is required — treat as a multi-step, medium-effort institutional route rather than a quick win.

### National Library of Jamaica — MEDIUM (institution)
- **What it holds:** Thousands of digitized historical Jamaican photographs, prints, and drawings, mostly freely viewable online already.
- **Reuse/licensing stance:** Governed by the Jamaica Copyright Act (1985); research/study duplication is permitted, but exhibition/publication use is decided **case by case**, and the NLJ explicitly reserves the right to refuse reproduction of any item. ([nlj.gov.jm](https://nlj.gov.jm/digital-reproduction-policy-2/))
- **Public contact route:** nljresearch@nlj.gov.jm (Research and Information Branch). ([nlj.gov.jm](https://nlj.gov.jm/faq/))
- **Reason for rank:** A real, working request channel exists, and much of its Jamaican music-history material is already digitized, but the case-by-case/refusal-reserved policy means outcomes are unpredictable — worth trying for specific named images once the institution's photo-collection catalogue is checked for reggae/ska-era content.

### The Reggae Museum (thereggaemuseum.com) — MEDIUM-LOW (institution; owner-requested addition)
- **What it holds:** An online (with a stated intent toward a future physical) cultural archive covering reggae's evolution from precolonial Caribbean peoples through ska, rocksteady, roots reggae, dub, and dancehall, plus sound-system culture, fashion, album art, and visual identity. Named contributing/featured photographers and artists include **Rohan O. Henry** (portrait series of reggae's founding figures), **Joe Swift** (reggae/dancehall visual chronicles), **Devon Garcia**, **Garvin Gray**, and **Basil Watson**. ([thereggaemuseum.com/collections](https://thereggaemuseum.com/collections/))
- **Who runs it:** Operated by the **Reggae Arts & Culture Foundation Inc.**, with curation credited to **YardRock TV** and an advisory council; content is credited as developed with **The Reggae Institute** using museum best practices (oral history, archival research, visual documentation, scholarly interpretation). A related/possibly distinct legal entity, "Jamaica International Reggae Museum Inc.," also appears in nonprofit registries (GuideStar) — worth disambiguating before contact, since a similarly named "thereggaemuseum.org" also exists and may be a different or affiliated project. ([guidestar.org](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/82-3071269), [thereggaemuseum.org](https://www.thereggaemuseum.org/))
- **Reuse/licensing stance:** No explicit reproduction, licensing, or Creative Commons policy is stated on the public site. The site does advertise a **"Contribute Materials"** pathway, suggesting it actively solicits donated/loaned archival content from the public and photographers — a sign of an open, community-building posture even without a formal published license.
- **Community/open project record:** Not confirmed beyond the "Contribute Materials" invitation; no explicit evidence of past open-licensing or Wikimedia-style donation work was found in this pass.
- **Agency entanglement:** None found; appears independent of Getty/Redferns/Alamy-style stock agencies, consistent with a mission-driven, donation-seeking model.
- **Public contact route:** Contact form at thereggaemuseum.com/contact/; no direct email, phone, or postal address surfaced in this pass.
- **Reason for rank:** A mission-aligned, actively-collecting cultural institution with named photographers already credited on its own site, which makes it a plausible single point of contact for several reggae-era images at once — but the lack of any stated reuse policy, and the unresolved question of whether it is the same entity as "thereggaemuseum.org" or "Jamaica International Reggae Museum Inc.," means this needs a clarifying email before any specific ask.

## DC hardcore

### DC Public Library — DC Punk Archive — HIGH (institution)
- **What it holds:** Est. 2014, an intentionally broad collection (1976–present) of DC punk/hardcore photographs, zines, books, articles, recordings, oral histories, and ephemera (fliers, posters, tickets); many originals were donated directly by scene participants (Cynthia Connolly co-founded the related Dischord archive; the collection has ties to UMD Special Collections too). ([digdc.dclibrary.org](https://thepeoplesarchive.dclibrary.org/repositories/2/digital_objects/1307), [americanlibrariesmagazine.org](http://americanlibrariesmagazine.org/2017/05/01/punk-at-dc-public-library/))
- **Who runs it:** DC Public Library, a public institution ("The People's Archive" / Dig DC digital repository).
- **Reuse/licensing stance:** Mixed by item — some items are explicitly marked "copyright undetermined," and for some DCPL Archives photographs, non-commercial use requires no rights-holder permission under applicable copyright law; but this varies item by item and is not a blanket open license. ([dclibrary.org](https://www.dclibrary.org/punk))
- **Community/open project record:** Explicitly a community-built, donation-driven archive; this is its founding mission, a strong precedent for a friendly, low-friction license request for specific named images.
- **Agency entanglement:** None.
- **Public contact route:** dclibrary.org/punk (collection overview, with library staff contact); digdc.dclibrary.org (digital repository, item-level "contact us" links).
- **Reason for rank:** Best institutional fit in the whole list — public library, explicit community mission, and a track record of photographers donating originals to it. A single request could plausibly cover several named images at once, and library staff are generally receptive to a credited, non-commercial reuse ask that matches the archive's own founding purpose.

## Garage / mod / Nuggets-era
